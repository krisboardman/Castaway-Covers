Grill Island Cover Calculator

Loose drape-style cover for a built-in grill island. Treats the island like a table: material spans the top (over the grill and faucet) and drapes down on the front, sides, and back. Snap corners + bungees, same as the other furniture covers.
Inputs — Island Footprint
The cover drapes loosely over the top of the island. H_top is the tallest thing sticking up off the counter (closed grill lid or faucet, whichever is taller) — the cover adds H_top of slack in each axis so the fabric can tent over it without pulling the corners in. The faucet just pokes up against the underside of the fabric; there's no slit.
Inputs — Drapes
The material spans across the top of the bar back and then hangs straight down off the back face. Back drape is typically shorter than the front/sides because the bar back sits higher off the ground. (Bar back at 43"? A 10" back drape leaves a 33" gap at the floor.)
Back Table Line
When a table butts up against the back of the island, the back drape hangs freely on the left and right but lays flat on the table in the middle. Enter the distance from each outer island edge to the nearest table edge. Two vertical reference lines will appear on the back drape in the assembly diagram — these are not cut, they just mark where each table edge sits under the drape.
Layout & Seams
When the cover is too wide for the bolt, it has to be made from multiple strips joined by seams. Long-ways splits the cover into 2 strips that span the full long axis — one seam, more bolt used. Short-ways splits into 3+ strips that span the short axis — more seams but less bolt. Comparison appears in Results.

Seam overlap is the total fabric consumed per seam, split evenly between the two strips. The default 1.5" means 0.75" from each piece of material — enough for a top stitch with the hem folded under. Bump it up if you want a wider overlap.
Grommets & Bungees
G1–G4 labeled on assembly diagram. Right side mirrors left.
Corner Snaps
Same diagonal-cut corners as the table cover. 1 snap set per corner — one stud half and one socket half that press together across the flap (2 pieces per corner, 8 pieces total).
